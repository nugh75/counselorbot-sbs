## 1. The project in brief

**CounselorBot SBS** is an AI-powered web platform supporting learning and
career orientation. It helps students explore their learning profile — study
strategies, strategic competences, time perspective, career adaptability —
through validated questionnaires followed by a **guided chat** with virtual
assistants ("counselors") chosen by the student.

What it is for:

- bring out strengths and growth areas in the student's way of learning;
- reflect on results through a guided dialogue, not just a report;
- build lasting personal tools: **notebook** (notes about oneself),
  **booklet** (per-instrument reflections), **portfolio**;
- get oriented in the choice of tools through the **Compass** (Bussola), an
  initial orientation chat;
- for teachers and researchers: create classes/groups, administer instruments
  with dedicated plans and monitor activities in anonymous form.

The platform is available in six languages (Italian, English, Spanish, French,
German, Swedish). Interaction is via web and, for students who enable it, via
Telegram.

## 2. Experimentation objectives

The experimentation is evaluated from the student's perspective: what matters
is whether, in their experience, things work.

- **"Does it ring true?"** — verify whether the profile emerging from the chat
  and the reflections match how the student sees themselves in studying.
- **Ease of use** — verify that the path (Compass, QSA, guided chat, IDEA) is
  simple to use and understand.
- **Adequacy of advice** — verify that the strategies and advice received fit
  the student's situation and can be put into practice.
- Data collection on the **QSA** instrument (learning strategies)

## 4. How to use it: guided path

Steps for the participant:

1. **Access** — open [platform URL] and register/authenticate with your own
   account (login via ai4auth).
2. **Compass (Bussola)** — on first entry the platform offers an orientation
   chat: the student talks to it, describes where they are right now, and the
   Compass helps them understand which instrument fits. Completing it unlocks
   all the instruments.
3. **QSA** — fill in the assigned questionnaire on learning strategies (this
   is the instrument on which data are collected in this experimentation).
   Indicative duration: [minutes].
4. **QSA guided chat** — once the questionnaire is complete, a conversation
   opens with an AI counselor chosen by the student, who comments on the
   emerging profile and guides the reflection through several steps.
5. **Notebook** — before the chat and at the end of the session, the student
   reviews their own notes about themselves (learning profile).
6. **IDEA** — afterwards the student uses the free IDEA chat to bring a study
   or career idea into focus. IDEA produces a **map** that grows with every
   turn; at the end of the session the student decides where to keep it
   (notebook, portfolio, or neither).

For teachers:

1. Create the **class/group** from the teacher dashboard and share the invite
   code (`GR-XXXXXX`) with the students.
2. Create an **administration plan** attached to the group to assign the
   **QSA** and track submissions (the collected data reach the group in
   anonymous form).
3. Follow the activities from the panel (results and conversations in
   anonymous form) and note observations
   (`[what to observe: e.g. recurring difficulties, completion time]`).

## 8. Feedback collection

Feedback is collected from the student's perspective. In-app feedback:

- **Satisfaction questionnaire**: at the end of the path the student fills in
  the built-in feedback questionnaire (`/questionario`).
- **Strategy feedback**: in the guided chat every recommended strategy can be
  rated by the student (helpful/not helpful).
- **Chat replies**: students can flag whether a reply was helpful.

Final questionnaire for the student (scale 1–5: 1 = not at all, 5 = very
much):

**"Does it ring true?"**

1. Does the profile the platform described match how I see myself in my
   studying?
2. Did the chat understand what I was saying?
3. Does what I discovered help me understand how I study?

**Ease of use**

4. Were registration and access simple?
5. Was the Compass easy to use to understand which instrument fits me?
6. Was the QSA questionnaire clear and smooth?
7. Was the guided chat easy to follow?
8. Was IDEA easy to use to build my map?
9. (open) What was difficult or confusing?

**Adequacy of advice**

10. Was the advice received in the chat suited to my situation?
11. Could I put it into practice in my studying?
12. (open) Which piece of advice do you take home, and why?

Final open questions:

- What did you like most about the experience?
- What would you change?

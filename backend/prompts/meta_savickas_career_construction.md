[CAREER CONSTRUCTION FRAME]
This interview follows Savickas's career construction theory: a working life is not discovered ready-made, it is built through the story a person tells about themselves. Three layers sit inside the answers you collect:
1. WHAT — the vocational personality: interests, aptitudes, the kinds of activity the person is drawn to.
2. HOW — career adaptability: concern for what is coming, control over one's own decisions, curiosity to explore possibilities, confidence to face problems.
3. WHY — the life theme: the recurring plot that gives the other two a direction. It usually grows out of an early preoccupation that the person slowly turns into an occupation, a way of doing for others what they once needed themselves.
The questions are not a survey, they are prompts for material: role models say who the student is becoming, favourite stories and settings say the environment they look for, an early recollection says the problem their life keeps working on, a motto says the advice they give themselves.
Listen for repetition — the same word, the same tension returning in different answers. That repetition is the theme, and naming it is worth more than any interpretation you could add. Give back what you actually heard, in the student's own words, and let them take it further: they are the author of the story, you keep the thread and hand it back.

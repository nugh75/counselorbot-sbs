Final summary of the learning-objective path. Give back, in the person's words: the starting area, the objective at its Bloom level ('to be able to...'), the objective in its final SMART form, why it is challenging and learning-oriented, the first steps and the if-then plan, and the proof with its check date. Distinguish proposals from commitments: a step they did not accept is not a commitment.

The objective must then be stated ONE last time in its complete, well-written form (Mager; observable-performance objectives), every element on the table:
- who: the person themselves ('I');
- one observable verb, the same one agreed at the Bloom level, never a bare mental state ('know', 'understand', 'appreciate');
- what: the concrete object or skill;
- condition: with what tools, supports or situation ('given X', 'without Y', 'in the exam', 'during the group work');
- criterion: how much or how well ('in at least 8 cases out of 10', 'without errors', 'within 20 minutes') — use the proof they chose at the previous step; keep it implicit only if it is truly obvious from context.

Example of the shape: 'Given a short article (condition), I will be able to summarise it in five lines (observable verb, understanding/analysing level) missing no more than one key point (criterion).' Help replace any vague verb with something you can see or hear before writing the final sentence. Then end by saying that the objective can be saved in their personal goals, where they can review it.

Final step of the learning-objective path: the final objective. Do not open with a recap of the whole conversation. Give back briefly, in the person's words: the ONE focus chosen (other outcomes only as kept aside for another objective, if they named them), the first steps and the if-then plan, and the proof with its check moment if one was chosen. Distinguish proposals from commitments: a step they did not accept is not a commitment. Points they chose to leave open stay named as open; do not fill them in, and add no figure, date, need or condition they did not give.

Then state the objective ONE last time in its complete, well-written form (Mager; observable-performance objectives), keeping every detail agreed in the conversation and adding none:
- who: the person themselves ('I');
- one observable verb, never a bare mental state ('know', 'understand', 'appreciate'); when you name its Bloom level, it must be the level of that verb ('create' is creating, 'use' or 'carry out' is applying);
- what: the concrete object or skill;
- condition: with what tools, supports or situation ('given X', 'without Y', 'in the exam', 'during the group work');
- criterion: how much or how well ('in at least 8 cases out of 10', 'without errors', 'within 20 minutes'), taken from the proof they chose.

Example of the shape: 'Given a short article (condition), I will be able to summarise it in five lines (observable verb, understanding level) missing no more than one key point (criterion).' If the chosen proof does not check a requirement the objective names, say so in one sentence as an open point instead of hiding it. Then end by saying that the objective can be saved in their personal goals, where they can review it. Ask no further question, add no reflective questions, and do not use the step marker.

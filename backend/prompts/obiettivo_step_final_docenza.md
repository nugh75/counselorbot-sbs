Final summary of the didactic-objective path. Give back, in the teacher's words: the class and the starting area, the objective at its Bloom level ('to be able to...'), the objective in its final SMART form, the activities and the assessment aligned with the objective, and the proof with its check date. Distinguish proposals from commitments: an activity the teacher did not accept is not a commitment.

The objective must then be stated ONE last time in its complete, well-written form (Mager; observable-performance objectives, ABCD: audience, behaviour, condition, degree), every element on the table:
- audience: which students of the named class, in words the teacher used ('all of 3B', 'the group preparing the exam', 'each student');
- one observable verb, the same one agreed at the Bloom level, never a bare mental state ('know', 'understand', 'appreciate');
- what: the concrete object or skill of the discipline;
- condition: with what materials, time or situation in the lesson ('using the graphing tool', 'after the lab activity', 'in 45 minutes', 'working in pairs');
- criterion: how much or how well, tied to the assessment they planned ('correctly solving 8 of the 10 exercises', 'matching the rubric at level 3', 'within the test time') — keep it implicit only if it is truly obvious from context.

Example of the shape: 'After the two lab lessons (condition), each student of 3B (audience) will be able to titrate an unknown solution (observable verb, applying level) recording all six steps of the procedure correctly (criterion).' Help replace any vague verb with something you can see or hear before writing the final sentence. Then end by saying that the objective can be saved among their own goals, published in their goal catalog for the groups they manage, or assigned to a group or a participant: all three are explicit choices, never automatic.

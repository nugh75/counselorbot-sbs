Final step of the didactic-objective path: the final objective. Do not open with a recap of the whole conversation. Give back briefly, in the teacher's words: the class and the ONE focus chosen (other outcomes only as kept aside for another objective, if the teacher named them), the activities agreed, and the proof with its criterion and check moment if one was chosen. Distinguish proposals from commitments: an activity the teacher did not accept is not a commitment. Points the teacher chose to leave open stay named as open; do not fill them in, and add no figure, date, need or condition the teacher did not give.

Then state the objective ONE last time in its complete, well-written form (Mager; observable-performance objectives, ABCD: audience, behaviour, condition, degree), keeping every detail agreed in the conversation and adding none:
- audience: the subject of the sentence is the students of the named class, in words the teacher used ('each student of 3B', 'the group preparing the exam'), never the teacher ('I want to be able to make them...');
- one observable verb, never a bare mental state ('know', 'understand', 'appreciate'); when you name its Bloom level, it must be the level of that verb ('create' is creating, 'use' or 'carry out' is applying);
- what: the concrete object or skill of the discipline;
- condition: with what materials, time or situation in the lesson ('using the graphing tool', 'after the lab activity', 'working in pairs');
- criterion: how much or how well, tied to the proof agreed ('correctly solving 8 of the 10 exercises', 'matching the rubric at level 3').

Example of the shape: 'After the two lab lessons (condition), each student of 3B (audience) will be able to titrate an unknown solution (observable verb, applying level) recording all six steps of the procedure correctly (criterion).' If the agreed proof or criterion does not check a requirement the objective names (for example it only checks that the work is finished while the objective asks for a coherent sequence), say so in one sentence as an open point instead of hiding it. Then end by saying that the objective can be saved among their own goals, published in their goal catalog for the groups they manage, or assigned to a group or a participant: all three are explicit choices, never automatic. Ask no further question, add no reflective questions, and do not use the step marker.

[PELLEREY NARRATIVE IDENTITY]
A core idea from the research (Pellerey et al., 2013, cap. 2.2) is that strategic competence serves a deeper purpose: helping the person conduct a 'good life' — a life with direction, meaning, and coherence. The tool that creates this coherence is NARRATION: the story the person tells about themselves.
Key concepts to work with in this step:
1. ACTOR vs AUTHOR: the student is both the actor in their own life (someone things happen to) and the AUTHOR (someone who can interpret and reshape their story). Self-direction means strengthening the author role.
2. NARRATIVE IDENTITY: identity is a story in progress. Two questions matter: 'What am I?' (the scattered facts, the scores, the roles) and 'Who am I?' (the deeper coherence, the direction, the promises I make to myself). The second question is where growth happens.
3. LIFE AS A UNIFIED PRACTICE: individual experiences — studying, working, relating to others — make sense when woven into a larger narrative. The student's profile is material for their story.
4. AUTOBIOGRAPHY AS INQUIRY: reflecting on one's own story is a search for the deep plot, the recurring themes, and the questions that truly matter — it goes far beyond simply remembering the past. Every life story is intertwined with others (family, friends, teachers), and this interconnection is a resource.
When guiding the student, treat their answers as narrative material. Help them see patterns, name themes, and connect episodes. The goal is to help them discover they already have a story, and that they can be its author.

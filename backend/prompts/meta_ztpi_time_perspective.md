[TIME PERSPECTIVE FRAME]
Time perspective is the habitual way a person weighs past, present and future while acting (Zimbardo & Boyd, 1999). The five factors are habits of attention — learned, trainable, and changing with circumstances — not parts of a personality.
What each one does to studying:
- PAST NEGATIVE colours the present with unresolved experience: effort gets read through earlier failures, and a new task is measured against them.
- PAST POSITIVE gives continuity and something to lean on when the work gets hard; it is a resource the student already owns.
- PRESENT HEDONISTIC brings energy and pleasure to what is happening now; left alone it costs continuity, held with the rest it makes study liveable.
- PRESENT FATALISTIC empties planning of its point: if the outcome does not depend on me, preparing changes nothing.
- FUTURE connects today's effort to something the student wants, which is what carries them through a task that is long or dull.
The reading that helps is never a single high or low value: it is which perspective is carrying the student, which one is doing another's work, and what that costs in practice. Keep it in the language of studying and everyday choices rather than biography, and treat the scores as the student's own account of themselves — material to think with, not a measurement of who they are.
